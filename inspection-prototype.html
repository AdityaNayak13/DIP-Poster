Where this maps to the script

DIGITAL IMAGE PROCESSING · stages 1–6

Acquisition, enhancement, restoration — the camera capture, box-blur denoising, and unsharp-mask sharpening you see in stages 1–3 are exactly the enhancement/restoration distinction Person 2 explains: enhancement improves visibility, restoration recovers information lost to degradation.

Preprocessing & segmentation — stage 4 normalizes pixel values to a 0–255 range (the same idea as scaling 0–255 into 0–1); stage 5 applies real Otsu thresholding to separate the part from the background, isolating the region of interest.

Feature extraction — stage 6 runs an actual Sobel edge-detection filter and measures edge density, fill ratio, and contour regularity — the shape/texture/edge features Person 3 lists.

MACHINE LEARNING · stages 7–8

Classification — stage 7 combines the extracted features into a defect score. Here it's a transparent weighted rule (so you can see exactly why a part fails), standing in for a trained Decision Tree, Random Forest, SVM, or CNN in a real deployment, as the poster proposes.

Output — stage 8 is the digital decision: defective or non-defective, with a confidence value. In production this is the signal that would trigger a conveyor-belt rejection mechanism.

Because the threshold is fixed rather than learned, borderline samples can occasionally be misclassified — a useful, honest way to demonstrate the false positive / false negative discussion in the script.

About this prototype. Stage 1 is the actual nut photograph cropped from your poster (embedded directly in this page). Every stage after it — blur, sharpening, Otsu thresholding, Sobel edge detection, feature scoring — runs live on that real image data in the canvas; nothing is faked or pre-recorded. The classifier is a hand-tuned rule engine standing in for a trained ML model; for the real project, replace classify() with a model trained on your labeled dataset and report accuracy, precision, recall and F1 as the checklist asks.
